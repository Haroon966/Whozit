Whozit · decision record · 16 August 2026

The Slug Is the Contract

The design conversation happened. Fourteen decisions came out of it, and Whozit stops being an attendance product that also recognises faces.

The whole thing in one line

Whozit holds the math — face vectors keyed by an opaque scope string and an opaque reference — and the consuming product holds the meaning: names, roster, register, dashboards. Nothing in Whozit knows a child’s name, owns a register, or ever learns whether it was right.

Product override · 18 August 2026

Whozit is also a standalone attendance app. Names live on refs as non-unique labels. The daily register is served from this process. Identity remains (scope_key, ref_id) — two children with the same name stay two people.

01 What was decided

Grouped by what each cluster protects. The numbers are referenced from the work order in section 06, so they are worth keeping stable.

The boundary

D1 · D2 · D3

D1

The register belongs to the consuming product. Whozit does recognition only.

Aggregation rules differ per country and would otherwise become our problem. Deletes daily_attendance and daily_attendance_present.

D2

No names are stored. The caller supplies an opaque ref_id.

Fixes the merge bug at its root by making ref_id the only identity key — and keeps identifying data away from partially-invertible vectors.

D3

The caller supplies the label; Whozit never mints an identifier.

Minting creates a second identifier space both sides must map, and makes us the roster backup we explicitly refuse to be.

Templates

D4 · D7 · D8

D4

Match by max over samples, not a mean centroid. Per-sample metadata; individual samples deletable; enrol returns sample_count.

Averaging across pose, lighting and a year of growth pulls the centroid toward the middle of the embedding space — adding a profile shot can make frontal matching worse. sample_count is the one moment a name-as-label mistake is detectable, because caller intent and stored state are both present.

D7

Store the aligned face crop next to each vector, encrypted.

The only defence against a backbone upgrade. Turns it into a batch re-embed instead of re-photographing a country — and removes the need to run two models through a transition.

D8

Crop retention configurable per deployment, indefinite by default. Deletion atomic across vectors, crops and log rows.

A crop that expires before the template it backs is not there on the day it is needed. The per-deployment key makes program deletion a key destruction rather than a cascade you have to get right.

The answer

D10 · D11

D10

Recognise returns decision and evidence: matched, ref_id, score, margin, candidates.

Ground truth never comes back, so the response is the only place ambiguity can ever be caught. Simple integrators read matched; careful ones route low-margin faces to a human.

D11

Greedy one-to-one assignment across faces in a photo.

Sort every (face, ref) pair by score, assign if neither is taken. Ten lines, no scipy. Hungarian differs only where scores cluster — exactly where margin already sends the face to a human.

Isolation

D9 · D5 · D6

D9

Deployment-level isolation — one container, volume and key per program. No tenant_id.

Deletes the entire class of cross-program leak bugs by construction rather than by careful query scoping. Gives data residency for free, and settles durability: SQLite plus Litestream, per deployment.

D5

Keep an identified recognition log — ref_id, score, margin — with a TTL.

Chosen over de-identified metrics so a named complaint can actually be debugged.

D6

That log is internal only. GET /v3/attendance is deleted.

A queryable per-child event log is a register by shape. A team that finds one will use it, and the boundary erodes one convenience endpoint at a time.

Lifecycle & surface

D12 · D13 · D14

D12

scope_key lives on the ref; add single and bulk move.

Annual promotion becomes one UPDATE instead of re-photographing a population. Covers scope renames too.

D13

Reference client rebuilt with zero persistence, deleted later.

It must be structurally incapable of being deployed, or a program adopts it and we own an attendance product after all.

D14

One API surface. The global name-keyed gallery is removed; a stateless detect route is kept.

No external consumers exist yet, so this is cheap today and never again.

What was considered and rejected

  • A name as an optional non-unique label. A nullable label that a UI reads is a second roster with no invalidation path, across a boundary we do not control.
  • Consuming products retain the enrolment images. An unverifiable promise whose breach is discovered on upgrade day — the worst possible moment.
  • App-level multi-tenancy. Requires permanently maintaining the code that prevents cross-program leaks; D9 removes the possibility instead.
  • Postgres now. The argument was shared writers, and D9 removed sharing.
  • A memberships table separating identity from scope. Structurally purer, but its only extra capability — one ref in several scopes — is speculative. Stays available as a purely additive migration.

02 Where the boundary sits

Two costs accepted on purpose

Whozit is blind to its own accuracy. Ground truth is the teacher’s confirmation, and that now happens entirely on the other side of the boundary. Score distributions are observable; error rate is not. That is the price of the clean split, and it is why the default threshold has to be calibrated by whoever integrates first.

Whozit is not a roster backup. Lose your ref_id mapping and your galleries are unrecoverable. Ugly to write down, which is exactly why it is written down — the alternative was a name column quietly committing us to be somebody’s disaster recovery.

04 The scope key, and the annual cycle

05 The integration contract

Four lines that live in the contract rather than the code, because nothing inside Whozit can enforce them.

ref_id must be a stable unique ID from your system, never a display name.

Renaming it orphans that person’s entire gallery. A collision silently corrupts a template — and Whozit cannot detect it, because it never looks inside the string.

Call the bulk move at promotion.

Skipping it silently re-enrols your whole population a year later. It will look like a model regression, not a missing API call.

We retain enrolment crops indefinitely by default.

Your consent language must say so. The README and PRD are currently false on this point and must be rewritten before anyone integrates, because the operator’s consent wording is derived from them.

The threshold is yours to calibrate, on your own population’s photos.

0.35 is a placeholder. Nothing downstream will ever produce this number for you — ground truth stays on your side of the boundary.
